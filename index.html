Does Physics Live in the Activations?

The Decodability-Causality Mismatch in Video Diffusion Transformers

Simulated collision, projectile and rolling videos are forward-noised and passed once through a frozen DiT. Read: a linear probe on attention-pooled activations regresses the target; it transfers, for example from a ball to a can, after recalibrating its gain and offset. Write: a direction is injected during generation and optimized on the motion an object tracker measures in the generated video. Injecting the probe direction changes the motion unreliably or degrades the video. In a preliminary study on Wan 2.1, the tracker-trained direction turns the ball but barely predicts its motion, whereas a jointly trained direction does both.

Abstract

Video generators produce convincing motion, but how their internal representations of a physical state relate to their generated dynamics remains unclear. Across five video diffusion transformers, including two video world models, we show that linear probes recover motion-related quantities such as speed, momentum, and gravity from the activations. Several quantities remain decodable even at noise levels where probes applied directly to the model input perform at chance. Using the model's attention to focus the probes on the object of interest improves decoding accuracy, and after recalibration the probes remain predictive when objects and backgrounds change.

However, accurate decoding does not provide reliable control. Interventions along probe directions rarely produce the intended change in motion and can degrade the video, and decoding scores do not predict steering. A preliminary study on one model shows that a direction trained to steer the rolling direction changes it but barely predicts the motion. In the same study, adding a probing term to the training yields a direction that steers the motion and predicts it as accurately as a linear probe. Consequently, decodability and control are separate properties, each to be tested for the use it is meant to serve.

Key Findings

01

Decodable

Speed, momentum and gravity are linearly decodable from five video DiTs, including two world models, at noise levels where a probe on the model input is at chance. On average, pooling with the model's own attention beats a global probe.

02

Transferable

After recalibrating only gain and offset, probes stay predictive under new backgrounds, object shapes and object counts, and partly under a new viewpoint.

03

Decoding ≠ steering

Probe directions rarely turn the generated ball, and decoding scores do not predict which ones do. In a preliminary study, a jointly trained direction both steers and decodes.

Controlled Scenarios with Exact Ground Truth

We render 200 videos per scenario with the Genesis simulator, so every clip comes with exact physical labels. Each video is encoded, forward-diffused to a chosen noise level, and passed once through a frozen DiT while we record every block's activations. We study three general-purpose video generators (Wan 2.1, CogVideoX-1.5, Open-Sora 2.0) and two world models (Cosmos-Predict 2.5, LingBot-World).

Physical Quantities Are Linearly Decodable

Ridge probes predict each simulator quantity across blocks and noise levels. Instead of averaging all tokens, which dilutes the object with background and mixes several objects, we pool each token-frame with the attention sent from the object's tokens in the conditioning frame. Two baselines separate what the DiT adds: a global probe over all tokens, and an input baseline on the noised latent with a random feature expansion.

How We Probe a Video Model

The five steps from a simulated clip to a probe score.

  1. Ground truth. A simulated clip whose every frame comes with exact physical labels: velocity, momentum, gravity, angles.
  2. Encode. The model's own VAE compresses the clip into the latent video z0, in space and in time (about four frames per latent frame).
  3. Forward noising. The latent is mixed with Gaussian noise to a chosen level. We report it as SNR in dB, from −40 (almost pure noise) to +16 (nearly clean), so levels are comparable across models.
  4. Capture activations. One forward pass of the frozen DiT, conditioned on the clean first frame and an empty prompt; we record the residual stream at the output of each block. No video is generated.
  5. Linear probe. Each token-frame is pooled with the attention sent from the object's tokens in the conditioning frame, and a ridge regression maps the pooled activations to the target. We score R² with 5-fold cross-validation split by video.

The model's input already carries some physics: the noised latent zt and the clean conditioning frame zc. We therefore probe that input too, with a random feature expansion so it has as many features as an activation token. The slider sets the noise level.
